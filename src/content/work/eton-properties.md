Eton Properties is a multinational developer with operations across Hong Kong, China, the Philippines and Papua New Guinea, running residential, commercial, property management, coworking, retail and hospitality. I joined in May 2016 as Senior Manager, Sales & Marketing and left in June 2023 as Director, having spent the last three and a half years running the function. The portfolio was over a thousand residential and commercial units, sold and leased to both businesses and individuals. Two very different audiences reached through one team.

## What the job actually was

Eight marketing professionals, reporting to the SVP, Properties. The job was the ordinary shape of a functional lead: deciding what the team worked on, defending that against what sales wanted this week, and owning the number at the end of it.

The largest piece of work was the customer journey. The funnel was long, the drop-off was in the middle of it, and the redesign shortened the path and lifted conversion 50%.

## Rebuilding the stack

The marketing technology was the constraint underneath everything else. There was no automation platform, and customer data lived in a legacy CRM nobody trusted. I implemented a marketing automation platform, migrated all customer data to a new CRM, and retired the legacy system. Conversion went up 30% on the back of it. The migration is the part I would flag to anyone who has not done one: moving the data is the easy half, and the hard half is that every process and every person's habits are built on the system you are removing.

Targeted digital campaigns across both audiences lifted lead inquiries 40%.

In the earlier Senior Manager years the work was more hands-on and the wins were more basic: website traffic doubled, lead generation up 50%, and virtual tours plus personalised email campaigns driving a 60% increase in customer engagement. The virtual tours turned out to matter more than anyone expected about a year later.

## minihotel, and what a rebrand does in a pandemic

minihotel is a Hong Kong boutique property, the hospitality piece of the group. Small rooms, very good location. I was stationed there for the period the group decided to update the brand, working with the Director of Sales and the Director of Hotel Operations. The rebrand wrapped just before COVID. Then international travel collapsed.

We kept the new identity and changed who we were selling to. With foreign travel off the table, targeting shifted to Hong Kong residents looking for a different room than the one in their own apartment. The rebrand was mostly strategy; the staycation pivot was mostly execution, with public-health restrictions moving under us the whole time.

![A piece of the staycation-era creative, with the brand's "much more than the size" tagline](/images/work/minihotel-hk-tagline.png)

The new identity carried the brand through the pandemic and the staycation positioning brought in local revenue. It is the clearest case I have of a brand built for one set of conditions holding up when those conditions disappeared entirely.
